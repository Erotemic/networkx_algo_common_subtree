## Exact counterexample and recurrence comparison (new, build pending)

`PrintedContainmentCounterexample.lean` proposes:

- `PrintedContains.one_pair_drop_top_level`: a one-pair deletion under the
  printed definition has an unchanged top-level decomposition.
- `nested_unique_top_level_decomposition` and
  `PrintedContains.one_pair_drop_nested`: a nested word has just one
  top-level deletion candidate.
- `branching_example_not_printed`: the smallest operational contraction
  `001011 → 0011` is excluded by the printed recursive relation.
- `printed_containment_not_complete`: the operational relation is strictly
  larger than the printed one.
- `dyckEmbeds_semilength_le`, `branching_example_printed_common_at_most_one`
  and `branching_example_operational_common`: the literal printed and
  operational common-subsequence objectives differ already on that pair.

**The Lean source has not been compiled here.** The proof statements are
new, have no intentional `sorry` or new axioms, and require local `lake build`.
Do not label them kernel-checked until the maintainer confirms a successful build.

`checks/check_paper_dp_recurrence.py` independently implements Lemma 8 and
compares it against both finite oracles from `check_printed_containment.py`.
For all 197 Dyck words of 0--6 pairs, 19,503 unordered word-pairs show:

- **0** recurrence/operational-optimum disagreements;
- **7,554** recurrence/printed-definition disagreements;
- the minimal pair `(0011, 001011)` has scores 2 (operational and
  recurrence) versus 1 (printed).

This is finite test evidence only, not a proof of Lemma 8.

Run:
