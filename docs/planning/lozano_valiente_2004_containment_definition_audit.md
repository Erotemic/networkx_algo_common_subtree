## Source and precise issue

Primary source: Antoni Lozano and Gabriel Valiente, *On the Maximum Common
Embedded Subtree Problem for Ordered Trees* (2004), Section 2, Definition 2;
Section 3, Theorem 2. Original PDF:
https://www.cs.upc.edu/~antoni/subtree.pdf

The paper defines balanced-sequence containment recursively using a relation
of the form

- base case: `s = t`;
- recursive case: `s = s1 s2 s3` and `t = t1 0 t2 1 t3`, with all six
  pieces balanced and `si` contained in `ti` for `i = 1,2,3`.

In Lean we represent `0 x 1` as `x.nest` and concatenation as `+`.
`Paper2004/PaperContainment.lean` defines the **least inductive relation**
`PrintedContains` generated by these two clauses. Independently, the existing
`DyckContractStep` deletes any matched pair within an arbitrary balanced-word
context; `DyckEmbeds` is its reflexive/transitive closure. The latter already
has a proved equivalence with operational ordered-tree edge contraction.

## First counterexample

```
Original:            0 0 1 0 1 1    = 001011
Delete the 3rd pair: 0 0 1   1       = 0011
```

The original word describes a root whose only child has two leaf children.
Contracting one of the edges from that child to a leaf produces the word
`0011` (a two-edge path). This is a valid tree-edge contraction and a valid
matched-pair deletion.

However, the literal recursive definition admits only these results from
`001011`:

```
001011, 0101, 01, empty
```

The operational edge-deletion definition additionally admits `0011`.
In the literal recursive clause, the only removable **top-level** matched
pair of `001011` is the outermost pair, whose interior is `0101`.
The recursive relation cannot turn `0101` into `0011` while keeping the
same semilength. Thus it cannot produce `0011` from `001011`.

The paper's Example 2 provides a larger consistency check. For the listed
word `00100100101111`, the literal recursion yields 11 outcomes; arbitrary
matched-pair deletion yields 34. The paper lists the larger family, consistent
with the operational interpretation. This supports treating the printed
recursion as incomplete rather than weakening edge-contraction semantics.

## Independent finite evidence

Run from the repository root:

```bash
python3 proofs/checks/check_printed_containment.py
```

The checker uses two separate recursive enumerators:

1. Literal `PrintedContains` grammar: recurse on balanced `t1, t2, t3`,
   combining independently contained subwords.
2. Operational deletion: delete any matched pair, then take the transitive
   closure over the resulting finite words.

For all balanced words of 0--5 edges, the checker confirms that the printed
relation is a subset of the operational relation, but the converse first fails
at three edges. It reports the number of input words with a discrepancy as:

| Edges | Balanced inputs | Inputs with disagreement |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1 | 0 |
| 1 | 1 | 0 |
| 2 | 2 | 0 |
| 3 | 5 | 1 |
| 4 | 14 | 6 |
| 5 | 42 | 26 |

This finite checker is not a formal proof. It supplies small regression cases
for the Lean theory and the later DP oracle.

## Independent tree oracle

The companion `proofs/checks/check_tree_contraction_oracle.py` builds ordered
rooted trees directly as ordered tuples of child trees. Its operations splice
child forests across a contracted parent-child edge and recurse below the
root. For all **197** trees of 0--6 edges (Catalan counts 1, 1, 2, 5, 14,
42, 132), it confirms equality between reachable tree-contraction outcomes
and matched-pair deletion outcomes after encoding. It independently confirms
`001011 → 0011` is a one-edge tree contraction. No Lean definitions are used
to construct the tree oracle.

This provides a checked finite regression baseline, not a general theorem.
The Lean-level soundness and completeness correspondence was established in
the prior compiled modules.

## Formalization decisions

- Preserve `PrintedContains` as the **literal least relation** generated by
  the two printed clauses; do not alter its meaning to force equivalence.
- Preserve `DyckEmbeds` as the contraction-based mathematical problem
  relation. It already corresponds exactly to `OrderedTree.Embeds`.
- Prove `PrintedContains.toDyckEmbeds` and its tree-level consequence.
- The follow-up `PrintedContainmentCounterexample.lean` proposes a Lean
  counterexample to the converse and an exact one-pair characterization.
  Its proofs remain **build pending** until the maintainer runs `lake build`.
- Check the paper's subsequent dynamic-programming recurrence against
  **operational** containment, rather than assuming the printed recursive
  relation suffices. Keep the paper's original statement in a comparison
  ledger, with the required correction made explicit.
- Preserve the independent small-tree contraction oracle, now exercised
  through six edges, and eventually give the enumeration a Lean-level
  correctness theorem.

## Follow-up: effect on Lemma 8

Paper Section 4, Lemma 8 gives a recurrence for longest-common-balanced-
sequence scores. Its third line explicitly contracts the first annotated edge
of one input when the first annotations do not both survive. That recurrence
is expected to agree with the operational pair-deletion semantics, rather
than the strict least relation generated by the printed recursive Definition 2.

The independent `proofs/checks/check_paper_dp_recurrence.py` checker evaluates
Lemma 8 and the two independent optimum functions on all 197 Dyck words of
semilength 0--6, comparing 19,503 unordered pairs. The recurrence agrees with
the operational optimum on every checked pair and differs from the literal
printed optimum on 7,554 pairs.

For the smallest example:

| Inputs | Operational optimum | Printed optimum | Lemma 8 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `0011`, `001011` | 2 | 1 | 2 |

Consequently, proof targets should use three separate, explicit names:
`PrintedContains` (the literal clause grammar), `DyckEmbeds` (operational
pair deletion), and `paperLcs` (the algorithmic recurrence still to be
formalized in Lean). The immediate mathematical work is the general
correctness of Lemma 8 against `DyckEmbeds`, including the structural
case split on whether the first matched pair survives.

Source: Lozano and Valiente (2004), Section 2 Definition 2 and Section 4
Lemma 8, https://www.cs.upc.edu/~antoni/subtree.pdf . The gap is specific
to the *least inductive interpretation* of the printed recursive clauses;
it should not be treated as a flaw in the operational objective or a
counterexample to Lemma 8 itself.
